# Period tracking apps other than Flo and Clue: user sentiment, pricing and fit (UK, 2025–2026)

> Research caveats, please read first: the network proxy blocked direct fetches of many key sources (Reddit, Wikipedia, Bearable, Marie Claire UK, unstar.app, naturalcycles.com). Most findings below therefore come from search-result snippets and summaries, not full-page reads. I did not find Wirecutter, Guardian, Which? or Independent 2025–2026 roundups in search results. Treat the figures as indicative and check the main ones (especially prices) before relying on them.

## 1. Most common praises and complaints per app

### Takeaway
2025–2026 roundups keep naming the same picks. **Apple Cycle Tracking** is best for wearable users, **Natural Cycles** is the only regulator-cleared contraceptive app, **Euki and Drip** are best for privacy, and **Bearable** is best for symptoms and chronic conditions. Simple free calendar apps (Period Calendar, Period Tracker) are rated highly on the app stores but reviewers rarely recommend them. **Stardust** is popular but criticised for being shallow, for a paywall on history and for poor privacy.

### Cited Findings

**Overall "most recommended" (2026 roundups)**
- In a 2026 roundup: Clue is the "best science-backed", Flo has the "most features", Euki is "best for absolute privacy", Natural Cycles is "the only FDA-cleared birth control app" and Apple's built-in Cycle Tracking is "best for wearable users". Bearable is best for linking the cycle to symptoms, mood and chronic conditions. — [Bearable, "Best Period Tracker Apps of 2026"](https://bearable.app/the-best-period-tracker-apps-of-2026/) (note: Bearable is a competitor, so its ranking is self-interested); [AllAboutCookies 2026](https://allaboutcookies.org/safe-period-tracking-apps)
- Drip, Euki and Periodical are named the "safest possible" trackers because they store data only on the phone, so deleting the app deletes the data. — [AllAboutCookies 2026](https://allaboutcookies.org/safe-period-tracking-apps)
- On AlternativeTo (a community-voted site), drip. is the top-voted alternative to both Flo and Clue (free and open source). Others listed are Mensinator, Menstrudel and Tyd: no login, no ads, no subscription. — [AlternativeTo: Flo alternatives](https://alternativeto.net/software/flo-period-and-ovulation-tracker); [AlternativeTo: Clue alternatives](https://alternativeto.net/software/clue--period-tracker/)

**Apple Health Cycle Tracking (iPhone and Apple Watch)**
- Apple Watch Series 8 or later and every Apple Watch Ultra use wrist temperature to estimate the likely ovulation day *after it has happened* (retrospectively) and to improve period predictions. It looks for the biphasic temperature rise that follows ovulation. — [Apple Support 120357](https://support.apple.com/120357)
- Requirements: wear the watch to sleep (Sleep Focus on for at least 4 hours a night, for 5 nights) and wear it regularly for at least 2 cycles before estimates appear. — [Apple Support 120357](https://support.apple.com/120357)
- It is not contraception or a diagnostic tool, and predictions are less precise when cycles vary widely. — [smartwatch-straps.co.uk blog](https://smartwatch-straps.co.uk/blogs/blog/can-apple-watch-track-menstrual-cycle-accurately) (low-authority retailer blog)
- Named "best for wearable users" in a 2026 roundup. — [Bearable 2026](https://bearable.app/the-best-period-tracker-apps-of-2026/)
- (Gap: I could not fetch the full reviews of its weaknesses. Commonly cited weaknesses are a basic symptom set, little educational content and iPhone only. These are unsourced here.)

**Stardust**
- 4.6★ from 28k+ App Store ratings, yet an analysis of 104,684 reviews classed 76.8% of the experiences described as negative. The two figures conflict. The second comes from an aggregator with an unclear method. — [justuseapp reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/1495829322/stardust-period-tracker/reviews); [unstar.app 2026 ranking](https://unstar.app/blog/flo-clue-stardust-apple-health-period-tracking-apps-ranked-2026)
- Price: free download; the full version costs US$2.99/week, $3.99/month or $24.99/year. Cycle history older than 6 months needs a subscription, and long-time users report frustration with this paywall. — [unstar.app 2026](https://unstar.app/blog/flo-clue-stardust-apple-health-period-tracking-apps-ranked-2026) (snippet only)
- Complaints: symptom logging is "intentionally simplified" and power users call it "shallow". Users with irregular cycles say it only works if periods are very consistent. — [unstar.app 2026](https://unstar.app/blog/flo-clue-stardust-apple-health-period-tracking-apps-ranked-2026)
- Likes: an astrology and "horoscope companion" feel, and social features that let you share with friends. — [Bustle review](https://www.bustle.com/wellness/stardust-app-review-period-tracking-astrology); [unstar.app](https://unstar.app/blog/flo-clue-stardust-apple-health-period-tracking-apps-ranked-2026)
- Privacy (brief): scored 2/10 from Mozilla, the lowest of the apps tested, for sharing birthdate, birth-control type, reproductive goals and symptoms with the analytics firm RudderStack. — [CyberInsider on Mozilla study](https://cyberinsider.com/mozilla-study-ranks-euki-as-the-most-private-period-tracker/); [Mozilla privacy review](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/pl/nothing-personal/stardust-privacy-review/)

**Period Calendar / Period Tracker (Simple Design, plus similar "simple" apps)**
- Note: several apps share near-identical names. "Period Calendar / Period Tracker" (com.popularapp.periodcalendar, by Simple Design Ltd) is the big Android one. "Period Tracker and Calendar" (iOS id1032267351, by Simple Innovation) is a different app. — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.popularapp.periodcalendar&hl=en_GB); [App Store id1032267351](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1032267351)
- The iOS "Period Tracker and Calendar" is rated 4.8★ from about 51.5k ratings and is free to download. Praise focuses on simplicity: quick to log the start and end of a period, and a calendar showing only the key data. It has added a "Perimenopause Mode". — [AppPricingLab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/1032267351); [AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/period-tracker/1032267351?country=au)
- Period Calendar is praised for its cute, playful, non-clinical design and easy navigation. — [AlternativeTo: Period Calendar](https://alternativeto.net/software/period-calendar/about)
- Mozilla privacy score: Period Calendar 6/10. — [CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/mozilla-study-ranks-euki-as-the-most-private-period-tracker/)
- (Gap: I could not verify the details of ads or the premium tier. These apps are generally ad-supported, with a paid upgrade to remove ads, but this is unconfirmed here.)

**Natural Cycles** (detailed in section 3)
- Strengths cited: hormone-free, works with Oura or Apple Watch instead of a morning thermometer, FDA-cleared. Weaknesses: costly subscription, needs discipline, 93% effective with typical use. — sources in section 3.

**Ovia**
- Recommended for PCOS and irregular cycles because "the AI learns your cycle" and it logs health metrics. Its fertility scores assume regular cycles, though, and "should be ignored with PCOS". — [PCOS Meal Planner, 2025](https://app.pcosmealplanner.com/knowledge-articles/any/best-ovulation-tracker-for-pcos-app-reviews-2025) (niche, low-authority source)
- (Gap: no UK price or 2025–2026 user-complaint data found. Ovia is US-focused, and much of its distribution is through US employers and health plans.)

**Glow / Eve by Glow**
- Eve focuses on periods, sex and mood logging, with a community feed and a fun, sex-positive design. Privacy is criticised (a "one star" privacy rating). — [The Flex Company blog](https://flexfits.com/blog/best-period-tracking-apps/); [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/best-period-tracker-apps/) (2023, dated)
- A claim that Flo, Clue and Glow are only "15–25% accurate" for PCOS cycles comes from a vendor blog with no visible method, so treat it as unreliable. — [ovul.ai](https://ovul.ai/flo-vs-clue-vs-glow/)

**Drip**
- Free and open source, with data kept on the phone. The interface is basic (Calendar, Charts and Stats screens) and supports logging bleeding, symptoms, cervical mucus and temperature, so it suits users who chart by hand using the symptothermal method. — [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/best-period-tracker-apps/); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/flo-period-and-ovulation-tracker)

**Euki**
- Mozilla's only 10/10 privacy score: it stores data locally, needs no account and has a 4-digit PIN. The trade-off is that it is "not the most intelligent", with no predictions and no reminders. — [CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/mozilla-study-ranks-euki-as-the-most-private-period-tracker/); [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/best-period-tracker-apps/)

**Bellabeat (Ivy / Leaf + app)**
- The Ivy bracelet is praised as the "prettiest fitness wearable" and links biometric data to the four cycle phases. It is criticised as expensive (about US$250) and as only partly delivering ("tries to do it all — but it can only do some"). — [TechCrunch review](https://techcrunch.com/2021/11/12/bellabeat-ivy-bracelet-health-tracker-review/) (2021, dated); [Inverse/Input review](https://www.inverse.com/input/reviews/bellabeat-ivy-wellness-fitness-sleep-tracker-review)
- (Gap: no 2025–2026 reviews found. Bellabeat rarely appears in current roundups.)

**Oura / Garmin / Fitbit / Samsung**
- Oura: in November 2025 the Cycle Insights update added 12 months of period and fertile-window predictions, with a model "designed to be more accurate" for variable cycles and for perimenopause. — [Oura blog: Cycle Insights update](https://ouraring.com/blog/oura-cycle-insights-update/); [Oura Cycle Insights](https://ouraring.com/blog/oura-cycle-insights/)
- Oura has a Perimenopause Check-In: a 12-question Menopause Rating Scale survey that produces an impact score and a PDF report to share with a doctor. — [The Flow Space](https://www.theflowspace.com/physical-health/prevention-longevity/midlife-wellness-wearables-oura-apple-samsung-3007689/)
- Oura claims 96.4% ovulation-detection accuracy across 1,155 ovulatory cycles (a January 2025 analysis). This is company-linked data, reported by a third-party comparison site. — [askvora 2026](https://askvora.com/wearables/best-for-womens-health)
- Garmin and Fitbit provide temperature, HRV and sleep data to third-party apps, but neither has published trials specific to perimenopause. A 2026 critique: "the sensors exist, the trials don't". — [the5krunner, Aug 2026](https://the5krunner.com/2026/08/31/menopause-wearables-sensors-no-trial/)
- Samsung: Galaxy Watch skin temperature feeds cycle predictions in Samsung Health, and Samsung has a Natural Cycles integration. — [The Flow Space](https://www.theflowspace.com/physical-health/prevention-longevity/midlife-wellness-wearables-oura-apple-samsung-3007689/) (limited detail found)

**Moody Month (UK-founded)**
- Positioned around mood and hormones: daily "hormone forecasts", wellness tips, coverage of perimenopause, pregnancy and postpartum, and integrations with Apple Health, Fitbit, Garmin and Oura. Paid tiers are monthly, annual or a one-off Lifetime membership. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1414905792); [AppFollow GB](https://appfollow.io/ios/moody-month/1414905792?country=gb)
- (Gap: no UK £ price or substantive user-complaint data retrieved.)

**Balance (Dr Louise Newson, UK)**
- Free; tracks perimenopause and menopause symptoms (brain fog, sleep, mood, joint pain and more) and periods, and produces a Health Report to take to a GP. — [App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/balance-hormones-menopause/id1503345959)
- Won Apple Editors' Choice and is ORCHA-certified for NHS digital health libraries. About 4.8★ from roughly 27k ratings. Some reviews complain about the cost of the subscription. — [App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/balance-hormones-menopause/id1503345959); [mwm.ai summary](https://mwm.ai/apps/balance-menopause-hormones/1503345959)
- The optional Balance+ subscription costs about $3.99–10.99/month or $49.99–98.99/year depending on region. UK £ price not confirmed. — [Balance+ page](https://balance-app.com/balance-plus)
- Some UK employers offer it as a menopause-support resource. — [Liberty Academy Trust](https://www.libertytrust.org.uk/page/?title=Menopause+App&pid=73)

### Inferences
- From the available sources, user sentiment splits along two lines: simplicity versus depth (Period Calendar and Stardust versus Clue and Bearable), and free with ads or a paywall versus paid but clean. Wearable-based tracking (Apple, Oura) is the main 2025–2026 trend and is rated well by reviewers for the low effort it needs.
- Reddit consensus could not be read directly. Recurring signals on AlternativeTo and in roundups point to drip., Euki and Apple Health for people leaving Flo for privacy reasons, and to Natural Cycles plus Oura or Apple Watch for contraception.

### Gaps
- Reddit threads could not be fetched (domain blocked). Reddit sentiment is inferred from AlternativeTo and secondary sources only.
- No Wirecutter, Guardian, Which?, Independent, Healthline or Verywell 2025–2026 pieces surfaced in search.
- App-store complaint breakdowns are missing for Apple Cycle Tracking, Garmin, Fitbit, Samsung Health, Ovia and Moody Month.

## 2. Which are free, ad-free or subscription; UK prices

### Takeaway
Free and ad-free: Apple Health, Drip, Euki, Tyd, Mensinator, and the Samsung, Garmin and Fitbit cycle features (all need no extra subscription for basic tracking). Freemium: Stardust, Period Calendar, Balance, Moody Month, Ovia and Glow/Eve. Subscription required: Natural Cycles. Oura needs a membership on top of the ring. Most £ prices could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- Natural Cycles UK: about £89.99/year including a thermometer, or about £12.99/month. Another figure quoted is £7.99/month or £99/year, with a 20% discount on annual plans. These figures come from aggregator and discount sites and disagree, so check them in the app. — [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-natural-cycles-cost/); [codes.co.uk](https://www.codes.co.uk/natural-cycles)
- Stardust: US$2.99/week, $3.99/month or $24.99/year; history older than 6 months is paywalled. — [unstar.app](https://unstar.app/blog/flo-clue-stardust-apple-health-period-tracking-apps-ranked-2026)
- Balance: core app free; Balance+ about $3.99–10.99/month or $49.99–98.99/year depending on region. — [Balance+](https://balance-app.com/balance-plus); [App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/balance-hormones-menopause/id1503345959)
- Moody Month: monthly, annual and Lifetime options. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1414905792)
- Tyd says it will "never collect or sell your data, require you to log in, show you ads, or sell you a subscription". Drip, Mensinator and Menstrudel are free and open source. — [AlternativeTo: Tyd](https://alternativeto.net/software/tyd/?p=2)

- Apple Cycle Tracking is free with an iPhone. The temperature-based ovulation estimate needs an Apple Watch Series 8 or later, or an Ultra.
- Oura's cycle features need the ring plus its monthly membership. The £ price was not retrieved here, so verify it.

### Gaps

## 3. Natural Cycles: UK regulatory and NHS status, cost, integrations, effectiveness controversy

### Takeaway
Natural Cycles is a certified medical device: CE-marked in the EU since 2017 and FDA-cleared in the US. It is **not available on the NHS**: NICE said more evidence is needed. It works with a basal thermometer, Oura Ring, Apple Watch or Samsung devices. The company claims the same 93% typical-use and 98% perfect-use effectiveness with each. In the UK it has a history of a misleading-advert ruling by the ASA, after a Swedish hospital reported unintended pregnancies.

### Cited Findings
- EU certification: CE-marked as a medical device ("digital contraception") in 2017. — [TechCrunch 2017](https://techcrunch.com/2017/11/09/natural-cycles-gets-30m-for-its-eu-certified-digital-contraception/?ncid=rss)
- Described as "the only FDA-cleared birth control app". — [Bearable 2026](https://bearable.app/the-best-period-tracker-apps-of-2026/); [AllAboutCookies](https://allaboutcookies.org/safe-period-tracking-apps)
- NHS: you cannot get it on the NHS. A NICE Medtech Innovation Briefing (MIB244) concluded more evidence is needed before NHS adoption. — [Pulse Today](https://www.pulsetoday.co.uk/news/clinical-areas/sexual-health-and-gynaecology/nice-more-evidence-needed-before-nhs-can-adopt-use-of-contraception-app/); [NICE MIB244](https://www.nice.org.uk/advice/mib244/resources/natural-cycles-for-monitoring-fertility-pdf-2285965632946885)
- Effectiveness: 93% typical use and 98% perfect use. The company says the same rates apply whether used with Oura or Apple Watch, citing FDA review of its clinical data. — [Natural Cycles: Oura](https://naturalcycles.com/research-library/how-effective-is-natural-cycles-when-used-with-the-oura-ring); [Natural Cycles: Apple Watch](https://www.naturalcycles.com/research-library/how-effective-is-natural-cycles-when-used-with-apple-watch) (manufacturer claims)
- Apple Watch Series 8 or later measures wrist temperature overnight every 5 seconds using two sensors. — [Natural Cycles: Apple Watch](https://www.naturalcycles.com/research-library/how-effective-is-natural-cycles-when-used-with-apple-watch)
- Device options (thermometer, Oura, Apple Watch, NC° Band and others) are listed on the devices page. — [Natural Cycles devices](https://naturalcycles.com/devices)
- Controversy: in 2018 the UK ASA banned a 2017 Facebook advert that called it a "highly accurate, certified, contraceptive app". The ruling said the 99% figure was perfect use and typical use is about 93%. — [TechCrunch 2018](https://techcrunch.com/2018/08/29/contraception-app-natural-cycles-facebook-ad-banned-for-being-misleading); [Stylist](https://www.stylist.co.uk/long-reads/contraception-options-nhs-pill-coil-condoms-implant-natural-cycles-app-hormone-free-birth-control-opinion-women/221522)
- A Stockholm hospital reported 37 unintended pregnancies among users, and Sweden's Medical Products Agency investigated. It closed the probe after Natural Cycles agreed to clarify the risk of failure. — [TechCrunch Jan 2018](https://techcrunch.com/2018/01/17/clinic-reports-natural-cycles-app-for-37-unwanted-pregnancies-since-september/?ncid=rss); [TechCrunch Sep 2018](https://techcrunch.com/2018/09/17/natural-cycles-contraception-app-told-to-clarify-pregnancy-risks/)
- Full Fact's explainer on whether it works. — [Full Fact](https://fullfact.org/health/natural-cycles/)
- The r/NaturalCyclesBC subreddit discusses Apple Watch use. — [r/NaturalCyclesBC mirror](https://lr.in.psf.lt/r/NaturalCyclesBC/comments/1erar4s/apple_watch) (content not read)

### Inferences
- Typical-use effectiveness of 93% is roughly comparable to the pill's typical-use figure, but far below the coil or implant. It suits people who are comfortable with some risk, or with using condoms on "red days".
- Linking an Apple Watch or Oura removes the need for a daily thermometer reading, which reviewers praise most. You still pay both the Natural Cycles subscription and the cost of the device.

### Gaps
- UK post-Brexit status (UKCA or MHRA registration) not confirmed in sources found.
- Exact current UK prices for 2026, including the NC° Band, could not be confirmed (official site blocked).
- No 2025–2026 independent effectiveness study found.

## 4. Best for perimenopause, PCOS/irregular cycles, simplicity and privacy

### Takeaway
- **Perimenopause:** Balance (free, UK, NHS-library certified, produces a GP report) and Oura (Perimenopause Check-In, cycle model updated for variable cycles). Period Tracker's "Perimenopause Mode" and Moody Month are lighter options.
- **PCOS and irregular cycles:** no app predicts well. Ovia and Bearable are suggested for logging; wearable temperature tracking (Oura, Apple Watch) gives evidence of ovulation that a calendar cannot. Ignore fertility scores.
- **Simplicity:** Period Calendar or Period Tracker, Apple Health, Stardust.
- **Privacy:** Euki, Drip, Tyd, Periodical (all store data locally).

### Cited Findings
- Balance: symptom tracking, a GP Health Report, ORCHA certification and Apple Editors' Choice. — [App Store GB](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/balance-hormones-menopause/id1503345959)
- Oura's updated model targets variable cycles and perimenopause; it has a Perimenopause Check-In with a PDF report. — [Oura blog](https://ouraring.com/blog/oura-cycle-insights-update/); [The Flow Space](https://www.theflowspace.com/physical-health/prevention-longevity/midlife-wellness-wearables-oura-apple-samsung-3007689/)
- Wearables have sensors but no perimenopause trials. — [the5krunner 2026](https://the5krunner.com/2026/08/31/menopause-wearables-sensors-no-trial/)
- Stardust struggles with irregular cycles. — [unstar.app](https://unstar.app/blog/flo-clue-stardust-apple-health-period-tracking-apps-ranked-2026)
- Ovia is suggested for PCOS, but its fertility scores assume regular cycles. — [PCOS Meal Planner](https://app.pcosmealplanner.com/knowledge-articles/any/best-ovulation-tracker-for-pcos-app-reviews-2025)
- Apple's predictions are less precise when cycles vary widely, and its ovulation estimates are retrospective only. — [Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/120357); [smartwatch-straps.co.uk](https://smartwatch-straps.co.uk/blogs/blog/can-apple-watch-track-menstrual-cycle-accurately)
- Privacy: Euki 10, Clue 8, Flo 7, Period Calendar 6, Spot On 5, Stardust 2 (Mozilla). — [CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/mozilla-study-ranks-euki-as-the-most-private-period-tracker/)
- Bearable is best for linking symptoms and chronic conditions to the cycle. — [Bearable 2026](https://bearable.app/the-best-period-tracker-apps-of-2026/) (self-promotional)

### Inferences
- For a UK user in perimenopause who already has an iPhone or Apple Watch, a likely low-cost setup is Apple Cycle Tracking for periods and temperature plus Balance for symptoms and a GP report. Oura suits those willing to pay for a ring and membership.
- The privacy-only apps (Euki, Drip) give up predictions and reminders, so they suit people who want a log rather than forecasts.

### Gaps
- No robust independent accuracy comparison for irregular or PCOS cycles across apps was found. The "15–25%" figure is unreliable.
- Samsung Health and Garmin perimenopause-specific features not detailed.
