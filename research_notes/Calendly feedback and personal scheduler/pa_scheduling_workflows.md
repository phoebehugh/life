# How EAs/PAs schedule on behalf of principals, and how booking links fit (or don't)

Research date: 2026-09-22. Method note: the environment's egress proxy blocked direct page fetches for almost every domain (Reddit, Medium, Substack, LinkedIn, Fortune, HN, Wikipedia, vendor sites). All findings below therefore come from search-result excerpts of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Quotes are as excerpted; where a claim is a vendor's marketing statement rather than an EA's own words, it is labelled **[vendor]**. First-hand EA/user statements are labelled **[first-hand]**. Third-party editorial/blog is **[editorial]**.

## 1. How EAs/PAs actually run scheduling (conventions, holds, invites, time zones)

### Takeaway
The dominant EA workflow is still email: propose a small ranked set of specific times (2–3 windows is the most-cited number; "3–5" also appears), place tentative "HOLD:" events on the principal's calendar for every offered slot, then convert the winner into a confirmed invite and delete the rest. Time zones are written explicitly and, when crossing zones, in both parties' local time; the EA (not the principal) is expected to own the follow-up, the final invite and, typically, the video link when their principal is the host.

### Cited Findings
- **Holds convention [first-hand EA tips, compiled]:** EAs "hold the spot by putting a tentative meeting on the calendar with the title 'HOLD: Meeting with Joe Schmoe'" while waiting for confirmation — [SnackNation, Calendar Management Tips From Scheduling Gurus](https://snacknation.com/blog/calendar-management-for-executive-assistants/)
- Placing a "holding" meeting on the executive's calendar is recommended, with a reminder set to either confirm or remove it — [The EA Campus, Effective Calendar Management](https://theeacampus.com/blog/effective-calendar-management/)
- Before confirming, EAs look at what sits either side of the slot and add a buffer after intense meetings — [The EA Campus](https://theeacampus.com/blog/effective-calendar-management/)
- **Number of options:** Prialto (managed EA service) guidance is to offer "two or three time windows" when proposing; for cross-time-zone meetings make the zone explicit or anchor in UTC, and ask the recipient to confirm before sending the updated invite — [Prialto, Meeting Confirmation Email](https://www.prialto.com/blog/confirming-appointments); [Prialto, Best Practices for Great Email Meeting Invitations](https://www.prialto.com/blog/meeting-invitation)
- "Offer 2–3 specific time windows upfront ... cuts the number of exchanges in half, and still keeps you in control of when the meeting happens" — [Overlappr, How To Schedule Meetings Across Time Zones](https://overlappr.com/blog/how-to-schedule-meetings-across-time-zones/) [editorial]
- **Ranked options across multiple calendars [vendor]:** EAs "can propose ranked time options to all parties ... place tentative holds on multiple time options, send proposed times to external parties, and convert the winning slot to a hard confirmation — automatically releasing the other holds" — [SchedulingKit, Executive Assistant Scheduling](https://schedulingkit.com/solutions/executive-assistants)
- "Assistants should offer times quickly in multiple time-zones and keep track of all the time they're giving out" — [Kristen Bullock, An Executive Assistant's guide to effective calendar management (LinkedIn)](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/executive-assistants-guide-effective-calendar-kristen-bullock) [first-hand EA]
- **Time-zone formatting:** always include the zone explicitly, e.g. "Tuesday at 10 AM EST / 3 PM GMT / 11 PM JST — does any of these work?"; list all zones when more than two are involved; confirm the final time in both parties' local time in the invite description — [Overlappr](https://overlappr.com/blog/how-to-schedule-meetings-across-time-zones/); [Prialto, Scheduling Meetings in Different Time Zones](https://www.prialto.com/blog/scheduling-meetings-in-different-time-zones)
- WonderCal's EA runbook advises storing an IANA time-zone name per participant, showing every proposed slot in each person's local time, and never carrying a zone as "UTC-5" across seasons because DST breaks fixed offsets; it also suggests recording "who carried the last inconvenient slot" for fairness — [WonderCal, EA Scheduling Across Time Zones runbook](https://wondercal.com/blog/executive-assistant-scheduling-across-time-zones) [vendor]
- WonderCal's runbook lists roles the EA still owns even with tools: "missing calendars, follow-ups, fairness, approval, and final invitation" — [WonderCal](https://wondercal.com/blog/executive-assistant-scheduling-across-time-zones) [vendor]
- **Who owns the Zoom link / invite:** Zoom's EA guide recommends the EA schedule directly from the executive's Zoom account rather than the EA's own, "so the executive can still own the meeting invite without having to do any of the heavy lifting on the back end" — [Zoom, Executive Assistant Streamlined Communication Guide](https://www.zoom.com/en/resources/executive-admin-best-practices-guide/) [vendor guide]
- Lead times: "for simple meetings, send your invite at least two to three days in advance; for bigger discussions like quarterly reviews ... one to two weeks ahead" — [Zoom EA guide](https://www.zoom.com/en/resources/executive-admin-best-practices-guide/)
- **Delegation mechanics (Outlook):** a delegate automatically receives Send-on-Behalf rights; invites then show "Delegate Name on behalf of Manager Name"; the option "Delegate only and send me a copy" means only delegates see accept/decline and the exec gets an informational copy — [Microsoft Support, About delegates](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/about-delegates-allow-someone-to-manage-your-mail-and-calendar-in-outlook-41c40c04-3bd1-4d22-963a-28eafec25926); [UVM KB, Delegate Access](https://www.uvm.edu/it/kb/article/delegate-access/)
- "Delegate Access is most commonly used between a manager and his or her assistant, where the assistant ... is responsible for processing the manager's incoming meeting requests" — [Virto Software, Outlook Delegate Access](https://blog.virtosoftware.com/outlook-delegate-access/)
- **Delegation mechanics (Google):** with Workspace delegation the assistant can respond to invitations on the exec's behalf, create/edit events, and receive change emails; "If your assistant is actively scheduling meetings, they need editing access. If they're mainly checking your availability before booking external appointments, view-only covers it" — [Google Workspace Learning Center, Delegate access](https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/168126?hl=en); [Google, Tips for administrative assistants](https://support.google.com/a/users/answer/9282973?hl=en); [Time etc, How to share calendar access with your VA](https://www.timeetc.com/resources/how-to-use-a-virtual-assistant/how-to-share-access-to-your-calendar-with-an-assistant/)
- ASAP (professional body) recommends documenting the executive's scheduling preferences in a scheduling SOP so a covering assistant is aligned "and you're not fielding unnecessary rescheduling requests" — [ASAP, Strategic Calendar Management for Executive Administrators](https://www.asaporg.com/articles/strategic-calendar-management-for-executive-administrators-1702938375834/)
- Workmate's EA playbook article frames the workflow as templates plus response SLAs plus edge-case handling (title only; page content was blocked) — [Workmate, How to Build a Scheduling Playbook for an Executive Assistant](https://www.workmate.com/blog/how-to-build-a-scheduling-playbook-for-an-executive-assistant-templates-response-slas-and-handling-edge-cases)
- Click-cost evidence for the manual workflow **[vendor]:** "Proposing times with holds used to take 77 clicks. With Vimcal EA, it takes 8, the rest disappear automatically" — [The EA Campus review of Vimcal EA (quoting Vimcal)](https://theeacampus.com/blog/review-of-vimcal-ea/)

### Inferences
- The EA's unit of work is "a set of held slots offered to one counterpart", not "a public availability surface". Any tool the counterpart offers should map onto that: a handful of concrete, time-zone-labelled options the EA can compare against holds already out with other people.
- Because EAs track "all the time they're giving out", a counterpart's tool that silently changes which times are on offer between the EA's first look and their reply will create double-offers; stability of offered slots for a stated window matters to them.
- The "Send on behalf" model means the EA expects to be a visible third party on the thread and on the invite, not a hidden operator.

### Gaps
- No accessible first-hand r/ExecutiveAssistants threads (Reddit blocked; site: searches returned nothing). The "3–5 slots" figure in the brief could not be sourced to an EA statement; the sourced figures are 2–3 (Prialto, Overlappr).
- Could not confirm a documented EA-to-EA norm on whose Zoom link is used when both sides have assistants; the Zoom guidance covers only the host-side EA.

## 2. What EAs say about receiving booking links (link vs. proposed times vs. shared view)

### Takeaway
The recorded objections to booking links are about hierarchy and effort-shifting, not usability: a link tells the recipient to do the work. EAs are described (mostly by third parties and vendors targeting EAs) as preferring to coordinate behind the scenes and present options rather than send links; a link is tolerated when the sender is the busier/senior party or when it is framed as a convenience alongside proposed times. Direct EA-authored statements were scarce in accessible sources.

### Cited Findings
- **The 2022 "Calendlygate" debate:** investor Sam Lessin called sending a Calendly link "a raw/naked display of social capital dynamics in business ... they are telling you that you are less important than them ... It's a 'Get in line' move" — [Fortune, Feb 3 2022](https://fortune.com/2022/02/03/calendly-outlook-social-capital-workplace-dynamics) [first-hand, senior recipient]
- "When you're the one asking for time, don't send a calendar link ... it moves all the effort onto the person who needs to book the meeting" — [Monday Morning substack, Calendly etiquette](https://mondaymorning.substack.com/p/calendly-etiquette) [editorial]
- Softening language recommended: "Here is a link to my calendar to make it easier to find a time that works for you" — [DEV Community, Scheduling links dos & don'ts](https://dev.to/elizabethwerd/scheduling-links-dos-donts-10-tips-for-better-etiquette-3p57) [editorial]
- LinkedIn posts titled "Don't send me your Calendly link, it's insulting" (2025) and "STOP sending Calendly links. START pasting your actual [availability]..." (Brandon Bornancin, 2025) exist; content not accessible — [LinkedIn, szspak](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/szspak_please-stop-sending-me-your-calendly-link-activity-7333876172302299139-52vn); [LinkedIn, Brandon Bornancin](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/brandonbornancin_stop-sending-calendly-links-start-pasting-activity-7396185403478708224-0VbH)
- "Scheduling tools promised to eliminate back-and-forth ... but instead just moved the friction from logistics to psychology"; a link starts the interaction with a "relationship tax". Same piece cites 5–8 email exchanges to book a meeting and "32% of meetings booked through scheduling links still get rescheduled" (stat provenance not given) — [AI Adopters Club, The Real Reason People Hate Your Calendar Links](https://aiadopters.club/p/the-real-reason-people-hate-your) [editorial; stats unverified]
- **How EAs are characterised [vendor targeting EAs]:** "Executive assistants typically don't send links or make people jump through hoops; instead they coordinate behind the scenes, maintain relationships, and present options that feel respectful to everyone involved" — [Sliq, Calendly vs AI Executive Assistant](https://www.trysliq.com/blog/calendly-vs-ai-executive-assistant)
- "In contexts like investor meetings, board member calls, and conversations with senior executives, the expectation is that someone coordinates on your behalf — proposes times, handles the back-and-forth, and sends the invite. A booking link in that situation reads as either impersonal or disorganized" — [Sliq](https://www.trysliq.com/blog/calendly-vs-ai-executive-assistant) [vendor]
- "Sharing a Calendly link with senior counterparts feels too transactional; a personalized assistant-mediated response is the norm at that level" — [The AI Career Lab, Calendly Alternatives for Executive Assistants](https://theaicareerlab.com/blog/calendly-alternatives-for-executive-assistants) [editorial/affiliate]
- Where links are fine: "sales demos, intake calls, and recurring 1:1s with colleagues" — [Sliq](https://www.trysliq.com/blog/calendly-vs-ai-executive-assistant)
- "When working with superiors, consider employing a more personal touch and take care of the scheduling yourself by coordinating directly with their assistant" — [MakeUseOf, Is Calendly Scheduling Rude?](https://www.makeuseof.com/calendly-scheduling-share-your-link-politely/) [editorial]
- Calendly's own position [vendor]: attitudes are "growing more favorable over time" and links are "not inherently rude" if framed courteously — [Calendly blog, Is sending a Calendly link perceived as less rude?](https://calendly.com/blog/scheduling-etiquette); [Calendly, Calendly etiquette](https://calendly.com/blog/is-calendly-rude)
- Calendly reports an 83% completion rate for people who click a booking link — [Calendly, State of Scheduling 2023](https://calendly.com/resources/guides/state-of-scheduling-report) [vendor stat; methodology not visible]
- One Capterra reviewer said Calendly "required too many clicks and its popularity made users appear lazy" — [Capterra Calendly reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/148036/Calendly/reviews/?page=16) [first-hand user]
- **Shared/overlay view as a middle path [vendor]:** SavvyCal lets the recipient "connect their own calendar and see both calendars side by side"; framed as "Calendly treats scheduling as a one-way flow ... SavvyCal treats it as a collaboration" — [SavvyCal, Calendly vs SavvyCal](https://savvycal.com/calendly-vs-savvycal); [Meetergo comparison](https://meetergo.com/en/magazine/savvycal-vs-calendly)
- Lex Friedman's "Calendar etiquette" post is a frequently-cited etiquette guide covering proposing times and links (content blocked) — [Lex Friedman substack](https://lexfriedman.substack.com/p/calendar-etiquette)
- VC Phil Morle describes using Calendly deliberately in venture capital, i.e. an example of the senior/busier party sending links — [Phil Morle, How I use Calendly in venture capital](https://philmorle.substack.com/p/how-i-use-calendly-in-venture-capital-c499b81088d8) [first-hand, sender side]

### Inferences
- For an EA, a bare link is a status signal about their principal, so the tool's job is to remove the "you do the work" framing: pair the link with concrete proposed times in the email body, and make the page read as "here is everything, pick or counter" rather than "book me".
- The most defensible pattern is the one Bornancin's post title implies: paste actual availability as text, with the link as an optional convenience.
- A link that shows lots of options and lets the EA counter-propose behaves more like a shared availability view (SavvyCal's positioning) and is less likely to read as a "get in line" move.

### Gaps
- No directly quotable EA (as opposed to executive/VC/vendor) statement on booking links was retrievable; Reddit, LinkedIn post bodies, and EA newsletters (The Assist, etc.) were blocked or not surfaced. Treat the "EAs prefer to coordinate rather than send links" characterisation as vendor/editorial consensus, not survey data.
- No survey specifically of EAs on link vs. proposed-times vs. overlay preference was found.

## 3. Tools built for or heavily used by EAs, and what EAs say about them

### Takeaway
The tooling splits into (a) delegation primitives (Google/Outlook), (b) EA-specific calendars (Vimcal EA is the only product positioned as "built for EAs", and it is the one with the clearest first-hand EA praise), (c) booking-link products with on-behalf features (Calendly Managed Events, Reclaim scheduling links, Cal.com team roles), and (d) proposal/poll tools (Fantastical Proposals, Calendly Meeting Polls). EAs' warmest recorded reactions are to features that shorten the manual propose-and-hold loop (drag-select times, copy as email text, auto-holds that self-delete), not to booking pages.

- ASAP's tool lists for admins mention Reclaim.ai and Clockwise for focus-time protection and Calendly for "daily meeting limitations" and minimum-notice rules — [ASAP, Game-Changing AI and Tech Tools](https://www.asaporg.com/articles/8-game-changing-tech-tools-for-administrative-assistants/)
- Skej markets EA use: "Executive assistants can collaborate with Skej to schedule for multiple executives"; works in email, Slack, WhatsApp, SMS, Teams — [Skej](https://skej.com/); [Skej blog](https://skej.com/blog/how-ai-assistants-help-with-meeting-scheduling) [vendor]

### Inferences
- The features EAs actually celebrate (Vimcal EA) are all on the *sending* side of the propose-times loop: select slots, copy as clickable text, auto-hold, auto-release. A counterpart's booking experience that fits this loop should let the EA *import* a proposed slot into a hold quickly (ICS per option, or clear times they can drag onto the exec's calendar).
- Calendly's on-behalf story is bolted on (book via your own page, forward notifications by filter); no mainstream link product has a first-class "I am the assistant, book for X, cc me" flow, which is the gap a PA-friendly page can fill.

### Gaps
- No first-hand EA reviews of Reclaim, Cal.com or SavvyCal in the EA role were found; their EA positioning is vendor copy.
- Vimcal EA's actual adoption numbers and any negative EA reviews were not retrievable (The EA Campus page blocked; only excerpts).

## 4. Lessons from email-negotiating AI assistants (x.ai, Clara, Julie Desk, Kono; Howie, Skej, Lindy)

### Takeaway
First-generation email schedulers (x.ai 2014–2021, Clara Labs 2014–2019 acquisition) were heavily human-in-the-loop, which made them slow (~10-minute latency) and expensive; recipients were confused about whether "Amy" was a person, resented email volume, and the bots broke on time-zone paraphrases and non-committal replies. x.ai was acquired by Bizzabo (June 2021) and shut 31 Oct 2021; Clara was acquired by TopFunnel (Aug 2019). Current products (Howie, Skej, Lindy) keep the CC-the-assistant model but lean on LLMs, keep humans as a safety net (Howie), and default to proposing ~3 slots.

### Cited Findings
- **x.ai timeline:** raised $9.2M at a $40M valuation early on — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1100512); ~$44M total from FirstMark and others — [Dennis Mortensen bio, evcrp.com](https://evcrp.com/); acquired by Bizzabo, announced 3 June 2021 — [GlobeNewswire press release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2021/06/03/2241277/0/en/Bizzabo-Acquires-x-ai-to-Launch-AI-powered-Scheduling-Accelerate-Personalized-Event-Experiences.html); final day of operation 31 October 2021, with Bizzabo choosing to fold the scheduling engine into its events platform — [Tasbia](https://tasbia.com/x-ais-scheduling-tool-is-shutting-down/?lang=en); [Carly, Calendly vs x.ai](https://www.usecarly.com/blog/calendly-vs-xai/)
- **Human-in-the-loop reality:** Bloomberg (April 2016) reported "behind almost every email was an actual human" — AI trainers like Willie Calvin; trainers "looked over parts of almost all incoming e-mails" before Amy auto-responded; x.ai said trainers still verified "the vast majority" of information — [Bloomberg, The Humans Hiding Behind the Chatbots](https://prod.cm.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2016-04-18/the-humans-hiding-behind-the-chatbots); trainers confirming Amy's predictions caused "the 10 minute latency in her responses" — [Chatbots Magazine interview with x.ai designer Diane Kim](https://chatbotsmagazine.com/amy-and-andrew-ingram-your-ai-assistants-from-x-ai-d350f5f58e43)

### Inferences
- The two recurring recipient-side failures — not knowing you are talking to a bot, and being spammed with re-proposals — are exactly the failures an EA on the other side will punish, because the EA is accountable for their principal's relationships. Disclosure and low message count matter more than cleverness.
- Time-zone breakage was about *paraphrase* ("12pm ET works" for a 9am PT offer). A PA-friendly tool should accept a counter-proposal in the counterpart's own zone and echo back both zones.
- The graceful-failure path (Amy "gave up" and told the user) is important: the tool should hand back to the human when the other side is non-committal rather than keep pushing.
- Every survivor/successor kept humans reachable (Howie's EA oversight) or kept the human user in control of tone (Skej), suggesting recipients/EAs value a human-accountable sender.

### Gaps

## 5. Features that would make a booking experience "PA-friendly"

### Takeaway
Combining the EA workflow (holds, ranked options, dual-zone text, EA-owned invite) with the documented shortcomings of link tools (notification gaps, forced self-booking, one-way flow) yields a concrete feature list: many options at once; propose-alternative/counter; book for someone else with the principal's email and the EA cc'd; plain-text availability the EA can paste; per-option ICS or drag-to-hold; easy reschedule; no account creation; explicit time-zone labelling.

### Cited Findings
- EAs want to type less: drag-select slots and "copy to clipboard" as email-ready text is the most praised Vimcal EA behaviour — [The EA Campus review](https://theeacampus.com/blog/review-of-vimcal-ea/) [first-hand EA]
- Auto-holds that self-delete once booked remove the "77 clicks" manual loop — [The EA Campus review](https://theeacampus.com/blog/review-of-vimcal-ea/)
- Booking on behalf via a public link today means the assistant enters the principal's "name and email" into the booking form — [Calendly Community](https://community.calendly.com/asked-answered-79/how-can-i-schedule-on-behalf-of-my-client-4669) — and Calendly then notifies only the host and the invitee, not the person who booked on their behalf — [Calendly Community](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/managing-calendly-on-behalf-of-others-1069)
- Ranking/preference ordering of offered times (sender's preferred windows surface first) — [SavvyCal](https://savvycal.com/calendly-vs-savvycal) [vendor]; overlay so the recipient sees both calendars — [Meetergo](https://meetergo.com/en/magazine/savvycal-vs-calendly)
- Proposal/vote mode with a shareable link, where the organiser locks the final slot — [Flexibits Fantastical Proposals](https://flexibits.com/blog/2022/02/fantastical-scheduling-arrives-with-version-3-6-openings-proposals-and-quarters/); manual-approval option for booking requests — [9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2022/02/08/fantastical-scheduling-openings-proposals-update/)
- Private event types with broader availability for manual/assisted booking so "you can schedule outside your public hours without exposing those times publicly" — [Calendly Community](https://community.calendly.com/asked-answered-79/how-can-i-schedule-on-behalf-of-my-client-4669)
- Show every proposed slot in each participant's local time; store IANA zones; never fixed offsets — [WonderCal runbook](https://wondercal.com/blog/executive-assistant-scheduling-across-time-zones); write "10 AM EST / 3 PM GMT" style — [Overlappr](https://overlappr.com/blog/how-to-schedule-meetings-across-time-zones/)
- Confirm the final time in both parties' local time in the invite description — [Overlappr](https://overlappr.com/blog/how-to-schedule-meetings-across-time-zones/)
- The final invitation and follow-ups are roles the EA expects to keep — [WonderCal runbook](https://wondercal.com/blog/executive-assistant-scheduling-across-time-zones)
- Exec-side EAs prefer the invite to be owned by the exec's account (Zoom scheduled from exec's account) — [Zoom EA guide](https://www.zoom.com/en/resources/executive-admin-best-practices-guide/)
- Reschedule volume is material: "32% of meetings booked through scheduling links still get rescheduled" — [AI Adopters Club](https://aiadopters.club/p/the-real-reason-people-hate-your) [unverified stat]
- Calendly's own data: 1 in 4 workers spend 3–4 hours/week scheduling — [Calendly State of Scheduling 2023](https://calendly.com/newsroom/press-release/state-of-scheduling-2023) [vendor]
- Booking-link "add guests"/multi-invitee handling and the ability to redirect to a teammate are standard team features — [Cal.com collective events](https://cal.com/scheduling/feature/collective); [Cal.com OOO redirect](https://cal.com/help/availabilities/out-of-office)
- Privacy: Fantastical Openings uploads only busy/free, no event details — [Flexibits](https://flexibits.com/blog/2022/02/fantastical-scheduling-arrives-with-version-3-6-openings-proposals-and-quarters/); exposing full availability to a senior counterpart is itself cited as a status/privacy concern — [The AI Career Lab](https://theaicareerlab.com/blog/calendly-alternatives-for-executive-assistants)

### Inferences (design recommendations derived from the above)
- **Lead with text, link second.** The email body should carry 3–6 concrete options written as "Tue 7 Oct, 10:00–10:30 BST / 05:00 ET", with the page as the richer view. This matches how EAs themselves write and neutralises the "get in line" reading.
- **Assistant mode on the page**: "I'm booking for someone else" → attendee name/email for the principal, cc field for the EA, and both get the invite; send a confirmation to the booker (fixing Calendly's notification gap).
- **Counter-propose control**: let the EA reply with their own 2–3 times (free text or picker) that flow back as structured options, rather than forcing a pick-from-list.
- **Hold-friendly exports**: a "copy these times" button producing plain text in both zones, and optional per-option .ics "HOLD:" files, so the EA can place holds on the exec's calendar in one step.
- **Stability window**: state how long the offered slots are reserved ("these times are held for you until Thu 5pm"), since EAs track offered time and hate re-offers.
- **No login, no account**: every accessible flow in Calendly/SavvyCal/Fantastical is account-free for the booker; a PA-friendly page must be too.
- **Rescheduling by the booker**: a manage link that lets the EA (not only the principal) move or cancel, given ~1/3 of link-booked meetings move.
- **Bot disclosure and restraint** if any automation emails the EA: identify as a tool, cap re-proposals, and hand back to the human when the EA is non-committal (x.ai's failure modes).

### Gaps
- No source directly tested "EA reaction to being cc'd on invite vs. principal-only"; this is inferred from Outlook/Google delegation norms and Calendly's notification complaints.
- No usage data on ICS attachments in EA workflows was found.
