## Key Question 1: What do reviewers on G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, Trustpilot, the App Store and Google Play consistently complain about?

### Takeaway
Across all six review sites the same five complaints recur: (1) calendar sync failures leading to double bookings (the single most-documented functional complaint on G2), (2) per-seat pricing that "feels fair for one user and steep across a team", (3) hard-to-reach, self-serve/AI-first support, (4) billing and cancellation friction (charges continuing after downgrade, non-refundable policy, forgotten seats), and (5) a navigation/UI that changed and became harder to find things in. Aggregate ratings remain high (G2 4.7, Google Play 4.4, Trustpilot ~4.0) so the negatives are a minority, but the Trustpilot 1-star cluster is dominated by billing/support rather than product bugs.

### Inferences
- The most damaging reviews are not about scheduling logic but about money and being unable to reach a human; a personal tool that is free or one-off priced, with transparent cancellation, removes the entire top cluster of 1-star drivers.
- Sync/double-booking complaints are structurally tied to Calendly's plan design (one calendar on free) and platform decisions (iCloud dropped), not just bugs — see KQ4.
- "Layout changed, can't find things" recurs on TrustRadius and Trustpilot; long-time users are hurt by redesigns of the admin UI.

## Key Question 2: The "sending a Calendly link is rude" debate

### Takeaway
The backlash crystallised on 25 January 2022 when investor Sam Lessin tweeted that Calendly etiquette is "the most raw/naked display of social capital dynamics in business" and that a link is a "get in line" move; it was covered by Bloomberg, Protocol, CNBC, Fortune and Fast Company within a week, and had precursors on Hacker News in April and November 2021. The core recipient complaint is asymmetry of effort and status, especially when the requester (not the requestee) sends the link, when the first slot is many days out, or when the recipient must fill a form and cross-reference their own calendar. The dominant softening advice is: ask first, offer to pick from their calendar too, list two or three concrete times alongside the link, and add warmth/flexibility language.

- Lessin's core argument as quoted by press: "When someone sends you a Calendly link and asks you to slot yourself on their calendar, they are telling you that you are less important than they / and that ALL of their current meetings are 'more important' than whatever you need them for — it is a 'get in line' move." — [CNBC, 7 Feb 2022](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/02/07/what-is-calendly-.html); [Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/90722342/calendly-twitter-branding-meeting-etiquette)

### Inferences
- The etiquette problem is a product-design problem in disguise: Calendly's booking page cannot (a) show the recipient a handful of curated times inline in the message, (b) let the recipient counter-propose, or (c) let the sender express "I'll come to you" — so the sender has to compensate with words. A tool that generates a message with 2–3 concrete times plus an optional link, and lets the recipient reply "none of these work, here are mine", would dissolve most of the backlash.
- The "first slot is 10 days away" grievance is a direct consequence of one-size-fits-all availability: the same link is shown to a VIP and a cold-caller. Per-recipient or per-link priority tiers are an unmet need.
- The debate was net-positive for Calendly's growth, which suggests the complainers are a vocal minority — but they skew senior (executives, investors), i.e. exactly the people a personal tool's owner most wants to meet.

## Key Question 3: Pricing and packaging complaints

### Takeaway
Complaints cluster around (1) the free tier's single active event type and single calendar connection, which push almost every real professional to pay; (2) per-seat pricing with no volume discount until 31+ seats and inactive seats that keep billing; (3) a steep Teams-to-Enterprise cliff for compliance features like SSO/HIPAA (~$15k/yr minimum); (4) two price rises (Standard $8→$10 in 2022, Teams $12→$16 in 2023); and (5) non-refundable billing plus charges continuing after downgrades.

### Cited Findings
- Free plan limited to one active event type; users "have to manually toggle the active event every time they want to switch between different meeting types." Community thread: "Only one active event type in free version of Calendly?" — one user "reported discovering the one-event-type limitation after a customer complaint, and felt Calendly wanted them to upgrade after damaging their activity and business without previous warning or information." — [Community 1102](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/only-one-active-event-type-in-free-version-of-calendly-1102); [Meetergo](https://meetergo.com/en/magazine/free-calendly) **[COMPETITOR]**
- Free plan includes one calendar connection; up to 6 calendars needs any paid plan; "on the free plan Calendly checks only one connected calendar. Bookings on any other calendar are invisible to it." — [Calendly help: connect your calendar](https://calendly.com/help/connect-your-calendar-to-calendly); [Cal Clear](https://calclear.app/blog/calendly-multiple-calendars-free.html) **[COMPETITOR]**; [Wellpin](https://wellpin.io/blog/free-calendly-what-you-really-get-free-plan-limits-and-better-alternatives) **[COMPETITOR]**

### Inferences
- The one-event-type free limit is the single most-cited "gotcha"; multiple sources describe users discovering it only when a client couldn't book. For a personal tool: never silently disable a link — fail loudly to the owner.
- Per-seat models make no sense for a personal scheduler; the resentment is about paying repeatedly for a commodity (see HN "NeetoCal is a commodity and is priced accordingly" thread) — [HN 36785707](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36785707)

## Key Question 4: Functional complaints

### Takeaway
The recurring functional failures are: calendar sync gaps causing double bookings (aggregated Outlook calendars, iCloud support withdrawn Aug 2024, Google sync errors); timezone mismatches (UTC fallback, device/VPN mismatch, confirmations showing the wrong hour); buffers and start-time increments interacting in unintuitive ways; availability rules that are per-event-type but not per-person or priority-tiered and cannot express "prefer" versus "allow"; reschedule/cancel being all-or-nothing and only reachable via email; notification gaps on mobile; and invasive calendar permission scopes.

### Cited Findings

**Sync and double booking**
- Community thread "Calendar Sync Failing, Getting Double Booked": user "reported major issues syncing with their Outlook calendars and repeatedly getting double booked... ### Inferences
- Double booking is mostly a *coverage* problem (which calendars are read, how aggregated/shared calendars are treated, iCloud absent) rather than a race condition. A personal tool should read every calendar the owner has, treat "Busy" as authoritative, and surface which calendar blocked a slot.
- Timezone bugs are largely about *display and confirmation*, not slot computation: the booking page zone, the email, and the .ics can disagree. Show the zone explicitly in every artefact and never fall back to UTC silently.
- Buffers/increments are a leaky abstraction: users think in "gaps between meetings", the product thinks in "clock intervals". Dynamic start times keyed to existing bookings is a clear unmet need.
- Availability rules are event-centric; nobody can say "for Alice show everything, for cold inbound show Tuesday afternoons only" without duplicating event types.

- I could not open any community thread, so reply counts, dates and staff explanations are missing.
- No source quantifies how often double bookings occur; all evidence is anecdotal.
- Found no user complaints specifically about "minimum notice" behaving wrongly (only buffers); may exist but not surfaced.

## Key Question 5: Complaints from people who receive links from others

### Takeaway
Recipients object to (a) being handed the work and the status hit (KQ2), (b) a first available slot days or weeks out, (c) having to open the link, cross-reference their own calendar in another tab and paginate month by month on mobile, (d) re-typing name/email/phone they already gave, (e) fixed durations with no shorter/sooner option, and (f) no way to counter-propose from inside the flow — plus, since 2024, a security reason to distrust unfamiliar links.

### Cited Findings
- Effort transfer: "it moves all the effort onto the person who needs to book the meeting." — [Chris Herbert, Medium](https://cgherb.medium.com/i-hate-calendly-meeting-invite-etiquette-96b318db4d5a)
- Feeling deprioritised by distant availability: "her next available opening is in 10 days, and you feel like your position on her priority list is somewhere below nine full days of people and activities." — [Bloomberg, 31 Jan 2022](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-01-31/calendar-etiquette-calendly-auto-scheduling-soctware-cause-grievances)
- Cross-referencing burden: recipients are "scanning a list of times and mentally cross-referencing their own calendar in another tab" (SavvyCal's framing of the Calendly experience; SavvyCal's fix is calendar overlay) — [SavvyCal vs Calendly](https://savvycal.com/calendly-vs-savvycal) **[COMPETITOR]**
- Mobile pagination: "clients complaining about the calendar format requiring them to click on each day to check availability on the Android app" — [Google Play (via search summary)](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.calendly.app)
- Empty month / next-month inertia: owners note invitees don't page forward when the current month shows nothing — [Community 3252](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/enhancement-request-re-dealing-with-view-next-month-inertia-3252)
- Confusion for the unfamiliar: "not so many people are aware of this app, so it can be a bit confusing for people you send your appointments to." — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/148036/Calendly/reviews/)
- Availability shown isn't real: "you can't pick any dates shown as available in reality" — [HN 26820318](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26820318)
- Repeated data entry: phone number requested up to three times because of separate SMS opt-in field — [Community 2305](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/how-do-i-avoid-asking-for-the-phone-number-3-times-2305)
- No priority/duration flexibility from the recipient side ("offering a slightly shorter time slot if available sooner") — [dev.to](https://dev.to/elizabethwerd/scheduling-links-dos-donts-10-tips-for-better-etiquette-3p57)
- Recipient refusal as a stance: "Some professionals refuse to fill in Calendly links... Some executives receive these from junior people and salespeople trying to sell them something and find it disrespectful." — [WSO](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/unprofessional-to-add-calendly-link-to-cold-email)
- Wrong-time confirmations land on the recipient: confirmation emails showing a different hour than the booking page — [Community 236](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/appointment-and-confirmation-displaying-an-hour-earlier-later-for-client-236)
- Cancelling requires digging out the confirmation email — [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/calendly/reviews/all)
- Distrust of links: 2024 malware campaigns abused Calendly event pages; Calendly added a "Report this event" flow for unwanted meetings — [Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2024/02/calendar-meeting-links-used-to-spread-mac-malware/); [Calendly help: report abuse](https://help.calendly.com/hc/en-us/articles/360055756454-How-to-report-abuse-or-unwanted-meetings)
- Calendly's own etiquette guidance implicitly concedes the recipient pain by recommending "let me know if nothing there works for you" — [Calendly blog](https://calendly.com/blog/is-calendly-rude)

### Inferences
- The "no way to counter-propose" complaint is real but appears mostly as an implied gap (people suggest working around it by email) rather than as a named feature request; no Calendly community thread titled "counter-propose" was surfaced.
- For a personal tool: (1) embed 2–3 concrete times in the message itself so the recipient can reply "2pm Tuesday works" without clicking; (2) let the recipient overlay their own calendar or paste their availability; (3) pre-fill known invitee details from the sender's contacts; (4) show the recipient's timezone unmistakably; (5) never present an empty first screen.

### Gaps
- Reddit threads (r/ExecutiveAssistants, r/sales, r/consulting) were not returned by search; I have no Reddit-native recipient quotes.
- No survey data on recipient drop-off rates or how often links go unbooked.

## Key Question 6: Why people churn to alternatives and what those alternatives claim to fix

### Takeaway
Churn drivers are price (free-tier limits, per-seat cost), the recipient experience (power dynamics, cross-referencing), lost integrations (iCloud), and wanting scheduling folded into calendar/AI planning. Each alternative attacks one of these: SavvyCal (recipient overlay, ranked availability, meeting caps), Cal.com (open-source, self-host, own your data, white-label), zcal/TidyCal (generous free tier or lifetime price, unlimited event types), Google Calendar appointment schedules / Microsoft Bookings (free with the suite), Reclaim/Motion (links that respect focus time and habits). All of these claims are vendor marketing unless noted.

### Cited Findings
- **SavvyCal [COMPETITOR]**: "makes scheduling collaborative instead of a nuisance"; recipient can "connect their own calendar and overlay it directly on the scheduling page, seeing both calendars side by side"; sender can "present availability windows in a particular order" and "limit how many meetings can be booked per day, week, or month." SavvyCal also says Cal.com is "still riddled with the same broken scheduling experience for recipients." — [SavvyCal vs Calendly](https://savvycal.com/calendly-vs-savvycal); [SavvyCal Cal.com alternative page](https://savvycal.com/cal-alternative-archive/)
- Third-party comparison: "SavvyCal focuses more on personalized and flexible scheduling options" vs Calendly as "a generic tool meant to appeal to a vast number of audiences." — [Efficient App](https://efficient.app/compare/savvycal-vs-calendly); [OneCal](https://www.onecal.io/blog/calendly-vs-savvycal) **[OneCal is also a COMPETITOR]**
- **Cal.com [COMPETITOR]**: "the open source Calendly alternative" where "you are in charge of your own data, workflow and appearance"; AGPL, self-hostable, "white-label by design, and API-driven for deployment on your own domain." Launched on HN as Calendso in April 2021 — the same thread that produced the "I hate Calendly" comment. — [Contabo blog](https://contabo.com/blog/calcom-vs-calendly/); [WZ-IT](https://wz-it.com/en/blog/calcom-open-source-alternative-calendly/); [HN 26817795](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26817795)
- **zcal [COMPETITOR]**: free plan "offering unlimited event types, full branding control, and basic team features at no cost" vs Calendly's single free event type — [zcal Calendly alternative page](https://zcal.co/calendly-alternative); [Koalendar comparison](https://koalendar.com/scheduling-software-comparison/zcal-vs-calendly)
- **TidyCal**: "simple booking pages with a one-time lifetime pricing option... when you want the core scheduling basics without a per-seat subscription feel." — [Lunacal alternatives roundup](https://lunacal.ai/compare/calendly-alternative) **[COMPETITOR]**
- **Google Calendar appointment schedules**: "started off as a barebones alternative, has gotten better over time. It's not as powerful as Calendly, but it's a reliable way for Google users to create booking pages"; free with any Google account; paid Workspace unlocks multiple schedules, reminders, paid bookings. — [Zapier](https://zapier.com/blog/calendly-vs-google-calendar/)
- **Microsoft Bookings** appears in every alternatives list as the "already included in M365" option — [Reclaim alternatives list](https://reclaim.ai/blog/calendly-alternatives) **[COMPETITOR]**
- **Reclaim / Motion [COMPETITORS]**: Calendly "only manages meeting scheduling... but doesn't block focus time, schedule habits, or optimize your time allocation"; Reclaim's links "only offer slots that don't break your schedule"; Motion at $19/month offers "a dynamically optimized calendar that reshuffles itself instantly." Framing: "Calendly is your front door... Reclaim is your back office." — [Reclaim](https://reclaim.ai/blog/calendly-alternatives); [Alfred](https://get-alfred.ai/compare/calendly-vs-reclaim); [Techno-Pulse](https://www.techno-pulse.com/2026/04/best-ai-scheduling-tools-in-2026-motion.html)
- **Apple-calendar users** are an explicit churn segment after iCloud support ended 20 Aug 2024; multiple vendors published "Calendly no longer supports Apple Calendar" migration guides — [OneCal](https://www.onecal.io/blog/calendly-no-longer-supports-icloud-calendar); [Meetergo](https://meetergo.com/en/magazine/calendly-apple-calendar); [Amelia](https://wpamelia.com/calendly-apple-calendar-alternative/) **[all COMPETITORS]**
- **Commodity pricing argument**: HN thread "NeetoCal, a calendly alternative, is a commodity and is priced accordingly" (2023) — [HN 36785707](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36785707)

### Inferences
- The alternatives map cleanly onto the complaint clusters: price (zcal, TidyCal, Google, Bookings), recipient dignity (SavvyCal), ownership/trust (Cal.com), and "availability that reflects how I actually want to work" (Reclaim, Motion). A personal scheduler's differentiation should be picked from that map rather than trying to out-feature Calendly.
- The recipient-experience gap (overlay, ranked times, counter-proposal) is the least crowded and the one most connected to the reputational "rude link" problem.

### Gaps
- No independent churn statistics or "why I switched" posts by end users were retrievable — every switching narrative found was vendor-authored.
- Reddit "which alternative" threads were not surfaced by search this session.
