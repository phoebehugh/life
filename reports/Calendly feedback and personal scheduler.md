# Steal Calendly's defaults, fix its recipient experience

Calendly's fans and critics are describing the same product from two sides of the link. Account holders rate it 4.6–4.9 out of 5 across tens of thousands of reviews for four things that are really one thing: a guest clicks, sees live availability in their own time zone, picks, and both calendars update with no back-and-forth and no double booking ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/calendly/reviews?qs=pros-and-cons); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/148036/Calendly/reviews/)). The complaints split into a money-and-support cluster that dominates the one-star reviews (billing after downgrade, AI-only support), a functional cluster (sync gaps causing double bookings, wrong-hour confirmations, buffers that behave unintuitively, availability rules that are per-event-type rather than per-person), and a reputational cluster, the "sending a Calendly link is rude" backlash that peaked in January 2022 and still recurs on LinkedIn ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/02/07/what-is-calendly-.html); [Community 1154](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/calendar-sync-failing-getting-double-booked-1154)). The wider landscape shows that every mainstream tool still reduces a calendar to a busy/free bit, that only SavvyCal exposes ranked "prefer, don't require" availability on the booking page, and that the AI layer has either resorted to humans in the loop (Howie, x.ai) or lost trust by moving things users considered fixed (Reclaim, Motion) ([SavvyCal Docs](https://docs.savvycal.com/article/20-ranked-availability); [The Split podcast](https://www.thespl.it/p/howie-the-ai-secretary-austin-petersmith)). Executive assistants, meanwhile, work in a different unit entirely: two or three concrete, time-zone-labelled options, tentative "HOLD:" events on the principal's calendar, and an invite they own; the features EAs pay for shorten that loop rather than replace it ([SnackNation](https://snacknation.com/blog/calendar-management-for-executive-assistants/); [The EA Campus](https://theeacampus.com/blog/review-of-vimcal-ea/)). For a personal tool serving someone with a 3.30pm school pickup, protected focus time, and evenings reserved for friends, the synthesis is clear: keep Calendly's reliable defaults, lead every outreach with pasted times and a link second, build an explicit "I'm booking for someone else" path, and replace the busy/free bit with graded, audience-aware availability that can explain why a slot is hidden.

## Read this first: the evidence is search snippets, not full pages

Every one of the four underlying research notes carries the same caveat, and it matters for how much weight to put on individual quotes. The research environment's egress proxy blocked direct page loads of G2, Capterra, TrustRadius, Trustpilot, the App Store, Google Play, Product Hunt, Hacker News, Reddit, LinkedIn, Medium, Substack, Calendly's own help centre and community forum, and most competitor sites. Findings from those domains come from search-engine result snippets of the cited pages: verbatim where marked, paraphrased otherwise, with surrounding context, reviewer role and exact date usually unconfirmed. Review counts and ratings are as indexed and lag the live pages; for example, Google Play appears as both 4.7 and 4.4 depending on which snippet was indexed when. Reddit content (r/productivity, r/sales, r/ExecutiveAssistants) was not retrievable at all, so there are no Reddit-native quotes anywhere in this report, and no first-hand statement from a working EA about receiving booking links was found; the "EAs prefer to coordinate rather than send links" characterisation is vendor and editorial consensus, not survey data. Only GitHub issue pages were read in full. Vendor and competitor claims are flagged inline; treat competitor comparison pages (SavvyCal, Reclaim, zcal, OneCal, Meetergo, Zeeg) as marketing that happens to be directionally consistent with user reports.

## Reviewers praise the absence of failure, not features

The praise is unusually uniform across platforms and years. G2 summarises reviewers as liking that "Setup takes minutes and invitees rarely need instructions", that "Calendar sync 'just works' and double-bookings are rare", and that teams "report cutting scheduling email threads to near zero" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/calendly/reviews?qs=pros-and-cons)). Capterra's **4.7 from 4,082 verified reviews** skews heavily to small businesses (**79% of reviewers**), and its recurring "what I like most" lines are about the recipient rather than the host: "Ease of use, not only for those using Calendly but to recipients of Calendly links" and "My clients always comment on how easy it is to book with me" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/148036/Calendly/reviews/); [Capterra p.11](https://www.capterra.com/p/148036/Calendly/reviews/?page=11); [Capterra p.2](https://www.capterra.com/p/148036/Calendly/reviews/?page=2)). TrustRadius rates usability, its highest-scoring attribute, at **10/10** ([TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/calendly/reviews)); Gartner Peer Insights sits at 4.6 from 383 reviews ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/market/scheduling-automation-software/vendor/calendly)); the iOS app is at 4.9 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/calendly-mobile/id1451094657?see-all=reviews&platform=iphone)). The highest compliment users give is inattention: a Google Play reviewer's "Set it up (simple to do) and don't think about it again" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.calendly.app)).

| Platform | Rating (as indexed) | What the praise is about |
|---|---|---|
| G2 | 4.7 | sync reliability, near-zero email threads, invitees need no instructions |
| Capterra | 4.7 (4,082 reviews) | client ease, professional look, blocking days/limiting times |
| TrustRadius | usability 10/10 | clean interface, real-time calendar updates, custom questions |

| Trustpilot | ~4.0 (~589 reviews) | positives on basic 1:1 booking; skews to billing complaints |
| App Store / Google Play | 4.9 / 4.4–4.7 | meeting info on the go, local-time display |

The specific features called out as delightful are the ones that remove a failure mode rather than add a capability. Automatic time-zone detection "is particularly appreciated for facilitating international meetings" ([G2 compare](https://www.g2.com/compare/cal-com-vs-calendly)); reminders are credited with a business outcome, with one multi-client freelancer saying 24-hour and 1-hour reminders "have significantly reduced no-shows" ([Workflow Automation](https://workflowautomation.net/reviews/calendly)); pre-booking questions mean the meeting starts prepared ([TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/calendly/reviews)); the browser extension and embeds put the link where the ask already happens ([chrome-stats](https://chrome-stats.com/d/cbhilkcodigmigfbnphipnnmamjfkipp)). Buffers, minimum notice and daily caps are praised as a category ("the ability to set up parameters to limit days and times") rather than by name, which suggests people value the feeling of control over their day more than any specific knob ([Capterra p.11](https://www.capterra.com/p/148036/Calendly/reviews/?page=11)). Two smaller signals are worth keeping. A former Calendly employee on Hacker News said the feature built in response to the rudeness critique, showing "availability both ways" when both parties have Calendly, was "one of the most powerful features built" ([HN 26820550](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26820550)). And a negative Android review reveals what recipients had silently valued: after a redesign, "clients are complaining about the new calendar format, saying it's too hard to find available days, they have to click on each day" ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.calendly.app)). Available days visible at a glance, few clicks, no account: the recipient's delight is invisible until it is removed.

The growth story confirms that the recipient experience is the product. Analysts describe Calendly's growth as "exposure virality — where user sharing is built into the product's main function", and Calendly positions itself against its own finding that scheduling a meeting takes "an average of 7.3 emails" (a vendor figure) ([OpenView](https://openviewpartners.com/blog/how-calendly-harnesses-plg-and-virality-for-growth/); [Calendly blog](https://calendly.com/blog/find-a-meeting-time)). Every booking exposes a new person to the tool, which is why the guest page, time-zone handling and no-login flow get the polish.

## The one-star reviews are about money; the functional complaints are about coverage and expressiveness

Aggregate ratings stay high, so the negatives are a minority, but they are structured. On Trustpilot the one- and two-star cluster is dominated not by scheduling bugs but by billing and support: a reviewer charged "$157 AUD over 8 months with only a partial refund after threatening legal action", another describing support as "AI or chat which is useless because all they do is throw lines upon lines of instructions", and Better Business Bureau complaints about being "charged even after downgrading to the free plan before the next billing date" ([Trustpilot p.2](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/calendly.com?page=2); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/calendly.com); [BBB](https://www.bbb.org/us/ga/atlanta/profile/calendars/calendly-0443-28174026/complaints)). Calendly's own community has standing threads titled "Charged after downgrade to free plan how to get a refund?" ([Community 5607](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/charged-after-downgrade-to-free-plan-how-to-get-a-refund-5607)), free plans get email-only support ([Calendly Help](https://calendly.com/help/troubleshooting-access-to-chat)), and G2 reviewers say "the price feels fair for one user and steep across a full team" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/calendly/reviews?qs=pros-and-cons)). The free tier's single active event type is the most-cited gotcha, typically discovered "after a customer complaint" when a link silently stopped working ([Community 1102](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/only-one-active-event-type-in-free-version-of-calendly-1102)). For a personal tool this whole cluster is free to avoid, but it carries one design lesson: never silently disable a link; fail loudly to the owner.

The functional complaints matter more for a builder because they are about the scheduling model itself. Calendar sync issues are "the most frequently documented performance complaint on G2 (46 mentions)" ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/calendly/reviews?qs=pros-and-cons)), and reading the threads shows this is mostly a coverage problem rather than a race condition: the free plan reads only one calendar ([Calendly Help](https://calendly.com/help/connect-your-calendar-to-calendly)), Outlook users who aggregate several calendars into one account report Calendly "doesn't recognize other calendar events even though they appear as 'Busy'" ([Community 1154](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/calendar-sync-failing-getting-double-booked-1154)), and Calendly ended support for new iCloud connections on **20 August 2024**, after which Apple Calendar users were left with an Apple-to-Google relay that competitors describe as having "8–24 hour delays" ([Calendly Help](https://calendly.com/help/icloud-overview); [OneCal, competitor](https://www.onecal.io/blog/calendly-no-longer-supports-icloud-calendar)). Time-zone failures are mostly about display and confirmation rather than slot computation: Calendly's own help admits that when it cannot determine the invitee's zone "the timezone on the invitation defaults to UTC", especially for Hotmail, Yahoo and AOL addresses, and a Capterra reviewer set up a Pacific-time event and "received an email confirmation for 4:30 UTC" ([Calendly Help](https://calendly.com/help/time-zones-overview); [Capterra p.15](https://www.capterra.com/p/148036/Calendly/reviews/?page=15)). Buffers are a leaky abstraction: users think in gaps between meetings while the product thinks in clock intervals, so achieving back-to-back-with-buffer requires setting start-time increments to the buffer length, "which looks messy on the booking page", and users have asked for "dynamic start time increments that relate to current bookings" ([Community 1072](https://community.calendly.com/featured-tips-tricks-32/start-time-increments-managing-your-booking-page-s-intervals-of-availability-1072)). The availability model is per-event-type, not per-person: nobody can say "for Alice show everything, for cold inbound show Tuesday afternoons" without duplicating event types ([Community 3391](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/changing-availability-based-on-meeting-type-3391)), and there is no "prefer versus allow" setting, which SavvyCal markets as its differentiator ([SavvyCal, competitor](https://savvycal.com/calendly-vs-savvycal)). Cancel and reschedule links are "an 'all or nothing' feature", reachable only from the confirmation email, which older invitees struggle with ([Calendly Help](https://calendly.com/help/how-to-include-cancel-and-reschedule-links-for-invitees); [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/calendly/reviews/all)). A reviewer objected that the app was granted access "to see, edit, share, and even permanently delete calendars, which feels far too invasive for a simple scheduling tool" ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/calendly.com)), and since 2024 recipients have had a security reason to distrust unfamiliar scheduling links after Calendly event pages were abused in Mac malware campaigns ([Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2024/02/calendar-meeting-links-used-to-spread-mac-malware/)). "Why is Calendly not showing my availability" has become a whole content genre, and Calendly ships a diagnostic tool for it, which tells you that stacked constraints (buffers, caps, notice, busy events across calendars) are genuinely hard to debug from the owner's side ([Calendly Help](https://calendly.com/help/how-to-troubleshoot-unavailable-times-that-should-be-available)).

### The etiquette backlash is a design gap wearing a manners costume

The reputational complaint crystallised on **25 January 2022** when investor Sam Lessin tweeted that "'Calendly' Etiquette is The Most Raw / Naked Display of Social Capital Dynamics in Business", arguing that a link says "you are less important than they" and is "a 'get in line' move" ([x.com](https://x.com/lessin/status/1486477359717187589); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/02/07/what-is-calendly-.html)). It had precursors on Hacker News, where an April 2021 commenter called Calendly "a technological solution to a human communication problem" and a November 2021 thread was titled "Is Calendly Rude?" ([HN 26820318](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=26820318); [HN 29260468](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=29260468)). Bloomberg captured the sharpest recipient grievance: "her next available opening is in 10 days, and you feel like your position on her priority list is somewhere below nine full days of people and activities" ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-01-31/calendar-etiquette-calendly-auto-scheduling-soctware-cause-grievances)). Wes Kao's widely shared explanation is that "when you ask your recipient to pick around your schedule, you're implicitly asserting a power dynamic, since traditionally the less powerful person accommodates the more powerful person" ([Wes Kao](https://newsletter.weskao.com/p/unspoken-power-dynamics-of-calendly)), and a Wall Street Oasis thread notes that some executives who receive links "from junior people and salespeople trying to sell them something" simply refuse to fill them in ([WSO](https://www.wallstreetoasis.com/forum/investment-banking/unprofessional-to-add-calendly-link-to-cold-email)). The backlash has not gone away: 2025 LinkedIn posts titled "Don't send me your Calendly link, it's insulting" and "STOP sending Calendly links. START pasting your actual [availability]" exist, though their bodies could not be read ([LinkedIn, szspak](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/szspak_please-stop-sending-me-your-calendly-link-activity-7333876172302299139-52vn); [LinkedIn, Bornancin](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/brandonbornancin_stop-sending-calendly-links-start-pasting-activity-7396185403478708224-0VbH)).

Two things about the backlash are easy to miss. First, it was commercially net-positive: "Overwhelmingly, people jumped in to defend Calendly", Marc Andreessen threatened to "permaban" from venture capital anyone who ignored his links, and Calendly reported "a surge in signups" ([Protocol](https://www.protocol.com/bulletins/calendly-twitter-debate?share_id=6887618); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/02/07/what-is-calendly-.html)). Calendly's own social listening put sentiment at "85% neutral to positive" (a vendor figure) ([Calendly blog](https://calendly.com/blog/scheduling-etiquette)). So the complainers are a minority, but they skew senior, which is exactly the population a personal tool's owner most wants to meet. Second, every proposed etiquette fix is a workaround for something the product cannot do. Kao's scripts ("Should I send over my Calendly if that's easier for you? Happy to pick from your calendar too") and Calendly's own recommended phrasing ("let me know if nothing there works for you") compensate in words for a page that cannot show a few curated times inline, cannot let the recipient counter-propose, and cannot let the sender signal "I'll come to you" ([Wes Kao](https://newsletter.weskao.com/p/unspoken-power-dynamics-of-calendly); [Calendly blog](https://calendly.com/blog/is-calendly-rude)). Chris Herbert's rule that a requester should "ask 3 days and times that work for them or suggest three 2 hour windows" is the human version of a feature ([Medium](https://cgherb.medium.com/i-hate-calendly-meeting-invite-etiquette-96b318db4d5a)), and a dev.to critique names the missing product semantics directly: "scheduling links not offering control around higher-priority vs. lower-priority meetings and inflexibility in links with set time durations that don't accommodate flexibility, such as offering a slightly shorter time slot if available sooner" ([dev.to](https://dev.to/elizabethwerd/scheduling-links-dos-donts-10-tips-for-better-etiquette-3p57)). The "first slot is 10 days away" grievance is a direct consequence of one-size-fits-all availability: the same link is shown to a VIP and a cold-caller.

## Every mainstream tool still reduces your calendar to a busy bit

The hard controls have commoditised. Calendly, Cal.com, SavvyCal, zcal, TidyCal, Google Calendar appointment schedules, Microsoft Bookings and Motion all offer per-event-type hours, buffers, minimum notice, per-day/week/month caps, date windows, date-specific overrides and multi-calendar conflict checks ([Calendly Help](https://help.calendly.com/hc/en-us/articles/32130092291095-How-to-set-meeting-limits); [Cal.com](https://cal.com/blog/booking-limits-frequency-duration-future); [Google Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2023/01/check-availability-multiple-calendars-appointment-schedules-google-calendar.html)). What none of them does well is the soft constraint this report's reader lives with: time that is technically free but not for this person or this kind of meeting.

The root cause is representational. Calendly "checks your connected calendars for events marked as Busy and treats those as conflicts; events marked as Free are ignored", with Outlook's Tentative, Away and Working elsewhere all defaulting to busy ([Calendly Help](https://calendly.com/help/how-to-manage-busy-vs-free-calendar-settings)). Google's richer primitives, the `focusTime`, `outOfOffice` and `workingLocation` event types, are "only available on primary calendars, and to some Google Calendar users", and only became readable via the API in November 2023 ([Google Developers](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/calendar-status); [Workspace Updates](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2023/11/calendar-api-read-write-out-of-office-and-focus-time-events.html)). Google's own appointment schedules cannot check a secondary or personal calendar for conflicts, per multiple community threads ([Google thread 358024536](https://support.google.com/calendar/thread/358024536/appointment-schedule-can-t-check-availability-on-secondary-calendar?hl=en)). The failure mode is symmetric, and Cal.com's issue tracker documents both directions: OOO events "incorrectly blocking out these times in my consulting service availability" (open since January 2024, marked low priority and stale) and, conversely, all-day vacations that Google marks as free and therefore get booked over ([cal.diy #13090](https://github.com/calcom/cal.diy/issues/13090); [Cal.com Help](https://cal.com/help/event-types/display-issues)). A user wanting in-person availability that follows their working-location days was told nothing better than to "create and maintain a separate Availability that matches my in-person days/hours" ([cal.diy #13979](https://github.com/calcom/cal.diy/issues/13979)). A binary flag cannot carry "bookable by some people, at a cost".

Three partial answers exist. SavvyCal is the only booking-link tool with first-class soft preference on the page: "Ranked Schedules" that "present your optimal times to meet first while offering additional availability as a fallback", a highlighted "Preferred Meeting Times" schedule on top of broader work hours, time blocks that define availability by the presence of specially named calendar events, and personalised per-recipient links whose availability overrides can be scoped "to a specific attendee" ([SavvyCal Docs: ranked](https://docs.savvycal.com/article/20-ranked-availability); [SavvyCal Docs: preferred](https://docs.savvycal.com/article/95-optimized-availability); [SavvyCal Docs: time blocks](https://docs.savvycal.com/article/27-time-blocks-to-define-availability); [SavvyCal changelog](https://changelog.savvycal.com/override-improvements-319737)). Reclaim expresses preferences as habits with a window, a duration range and an "ideally at" time, and gives scheduling links priority levels so that "Higher-priority links will show lower-priority Habits, Tasks, Smart Meeting events, and non-Reclaim events on the calendar as available time slots" ([Reclaim Help: habits](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/4129152-habits-overview-auto-schedule-flexible-time-for-your-routines); [Reclaim Help: priorities](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/8291694-how-reclaim-uses-priorities-to-intelligently-plan-your-workweek)). Vimcal and Notion Calendar take the manual-but-fast route: drag across free time to produce a typed list of slots plus a link, with auto-created holds that "self-destruct" when booked ([Vimcal](https://www.vimcal.com/calendly-alternative); [Notion Help](https://www.notion.com/help/availability-blocking-and-time-zones)). Differentiation by who is booking is done entirely through links rather than identity: Calendly single-use links that expire after one booking, Cal.com private links that can "expire after 2 successful bookings", SavvyCal personalised links, Reclaim priority links ([Calendly Help](https://calendly.com/help/how-to-create-a-single-use-link); [Cal.com Help](https://cal.com/help/event-types/private-links)). No surveyed tool reads the booker's email domain or contact group and adapts availability automatically; the "who" model is really "which link did I send".

The unmet asks sit visibly on trackers. A Cal.com issue opened on **24 December 2025** proposes "Adjacency-only booking", showing only slots that start immediately after or end immediately before existing meetings, with an adjacency tolerance, a filter for "any busy event" versus "only meetings", and an empty-day rule; the author wants to "batch all my meetings together to preserve large, uninterrupted blocks for deep work" and says Reclaim and Motion "maybe get me 60% of the way there" ([cal.com #26187](https://github.com/calcom/cal.com/issues/26187)). It has no maintainer response. Calendly users asked for the same batching-and-ranking years earlier ([Calendly Community #210](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/add-rule-to-limit-set-maximum-number-of-meetings-of-a-type-per-week-210)). A September 2026 request asks for "Maximum visible slots per day" that "should NOT reduce the real availability" ([cal.diy #30089](https://github.com/calcom/cal.diy/issues/30089)). The academic literature has argued for graded preference since groupTime at CHI 2006, which "investigates using a calendar to represent user preferences rather than availability" ([groupTime](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/2006/groupTime/groupTimeCHI2006.pdf)), and the Haze study at CHI 2022 found that digital calendars "leave little space to communicate uncertainty" and that people with "caring responsibilities" in particular "cover up their experiences of uncertainty" ([ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3491102.3502107)). That finding maps directly onto a school-pickup hard stop followed by a "probably free after 4.15 but don't count on it" band.

### AI assistants either move things you wanted fixed or need humans behind them

The AI layer splits into two preference models, and each has a characteristic trust failure. Structured-goal optimisers (Reclaim, Motion, and Clockwise until it was sunset in March 2026 after its Salesforce acquisition, per a competitor's account) are accurate about what they were told but lose trust when they act on things the user considered fixed: Reclaim users report it "automatically rescheduling meetings that are set for specific times" and adding "focus times and lunch blocks that didn't align with their existing schedule", with blocks that "couldn't be removed even after uninstalling" ([Trustpilot: reclaim.ai](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/reclaim.ai); [Reclaim blog, competitor on Clockwise](https://reclaim.ai/blog/clockwise-vs-reclaim)). Motion's auto-scheduling is praised for tasks but 2025–26 sentiment is described as "mixed to negative" over pricing changes, trials auto-converting to annual plans and a clunky UI ([Saner.AI](https://www.saner.ai/blogs/motion-reviews)). Email-negotiating agents face the opposite problem, inferring unstated preferences, and every one has resorted to humans in the loop. x.ai's Amy ran with **39 of 93 employees as "AI trainers"** verifying predictions, which caused roughly ten minutes of latency per reply; Bloomberg reported in 2016 that "behind almost every email was an actual human" ([Chatbots Magazine](https://chatbotsmagazine.com/amy-and-andrew-ingram-your-ai-assistants-from-x-ai-d350f5f58e43); [Bloomberg](https://prod.cm.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2016-04-18/the-humans-hiding-behind-the-chatbots)). Amy broke on time-zone paraphrase ("If she proposes 9am PT and you in New York ... respond back 'Yes 12pm ET works', Amy gets confused"), recipients "resented being bombarded by countless emails", and when a counterpart stayed non-committal Amy "eventually gave up" and handed back to the user ([Apoorv Agrawal](https://apoorv03.com/p/amy-ingram-a-product-review-601eb29acd2c); [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2015/testing-amy-what-its-like-to-have-appointments-scheduled-by-an-ai-assistant/)). x.ai was acquired by Bizzabo in June 2021 and shut on 31 October 2021 ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2021/06/03/2241277/0/en/Bizzabo-Acquires-x-ai-to-Launch-AI-powered-Scheduling-Accelerate-Personalized-Event-Experiences.html)). A decade on, Howie's CEO says mistake tolerance in scheduling is "really low" and the company runs "65 full-time contractors working around the clock as humans in the loop" ([The Split](https://www.thespl.it/p/howie-the-ai-secretary-austin-petersmith)). Gemini's "Help me schedule" in Gmail suggests times from calendar and email context but does not enforce preferences autonomously ([Google blog](https://blog.google/products/workspace/help-me-schedule-gmail-gemini/)). The lesson is not that AI is useless but that its safe role in a personal tool is to suggest and classify ("this request is from a stranger; offer Tuesday/Thursday afternoons"), never to silently move existing events or send unbounded email on the owner's behalf.

## PAs work in holds and ranked options, and links ask them to do the work

The executive-assistant workflow is still email, and its unit of work is "a small set of held slots offered to one counterpart", not "a public availability surface". EA-sourced guidance describes proposing "two or three time windows", placing tentative events titled "HOLD: Meeting with Joe Schmoe" on the principal's calendar for each offered slot, converting the winner into a confirmed invite and deleting the rest ([Prialto](https://www.prialto.com/blog/confirming-appointments); [SnackNation](https://snacknation.com/blog/calendar-management-for-executive-assistants/); [The EA Campus](https://theeacampus.com/blog/effective-calendar-management/)). Time zones are written explicitly and in both parties' local time ("Tuesday at 10 AM EST / 3 PM GMT"), the final time is confirmed in both zones in the invite, and an EA guide warns to store an IANA zone per participant and never carry "UTC-5" across seasons ([Overlappr](https://overlappr.com/blog/how-to-schedule-meetings-across-time-zones/); [WonderCal, vendor](https://wondercal.com/blog/executive-assistant-scheduling-across-time-zones)). One EA's advice is to "offer times quickly in multiple time-zones and keep track of all the time they're giving out" ([Kristen Bullock, LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/executive-assistants-guide-effective-calendar-kristen-bullock)), which has a direct consequence for a counterpart's tool: if the offered slots silently change between the EA's first look and their reply, the EA ends up double-offering time. The EA also expects to be a visible party. Outlook delegates send invites as "Delegate Name on behalf of Manager Name" ([Microsoft Support](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/office/about-delegates-allow-someone-to-manage-your-mail-and-calendar-in-outlook-41c40c04-3bd1-4d22-963a-28eafec25926)), Zoom's EA guide recommends scheduling from the executive's account "so the executive can still own the meeting invite" ([Zoom](https://www.zoom.com/en/resources/executive-admin-best-practices-guide/)), and the professional body ASAP recommends documenting the executive's scheduling preferences in an SOP ([ASAP](https://www.asaporg.com/articles/strategic-calendar-management-for-executive-administrators-1702938375834/)).

The strongest first-hand EA signal is behavioural: what they pay for. Vimcal EA, the only calendar positioned as "built specifically for assistants" at a reported $62.50 a month, wins praise for exactly the sending side of the propose-and-hold loop. An EA-community review says "you just drag and select the slots you want to offer. Vimcal EA instantly creates a polished, email-friendly message with clickable times, ready to paste straight into your email", holds those times automatically and "will delete the unused ones once the meeting is booked"; the vendor's own claim is that "Proposing times with holds used to take 77 clicks. With Vimcal EA, it takes 8" ([The EA Campus](https://theeacampus.com/blog/review-of-vimcal-ea/); [Temporal](https://temporal.day/blog/best-calendar-app-executive-assistants-2026)). An EA testimonial in the same review: "Every time I 'select availability' and 'copy to clipboard', I high five the Vimcal EA team mentally." Fantastical's 2022 Proposals feature (organiser suggests several times, recipients vote, organiser locks one) is the same loop in product form ([Flexibits](https://flexibits.com/blog/2022/02/fantastical-scheduling-arrives-with-version-3-6-openings-proposals-and-quarters/)).

Booking links fit this loop badly, and Calendly's on-behalf story shows why. To book for a principal, an assistant is told to open the public page and complete the form "with their name and email", ideally via "a private event type with broader availability" ([Calendly Community](https://community.calendly.com/asked-answered-79/how-can-i-schedule-on-behalf-of-my-client-4669)); Calendly then "will only notify the Calendly user that is booked with the event, and the invitee", so the person who actually did the booking gets nothing unless they set up an inbox filter ([Calendly Community](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/managing-calendly-on-behalf-of-others-1069)). No mainstream link product has a first-class "I am the assistant, book for X, cc me" flow. Vendors targeting EAs characterise the culture as coordinating "behind the scenes" and presenting options, and claim that with "investor meetings, board member calls, and conversations with senior executives ... a booking link in that situation reads as either impersonal or disorganized" ([Sliq, vendor](https://www.trysliq.com/blog/calendly-vs-ai-executive-assistant)); this is consistent with the etiquette literature but, as noted above, no working EA's own statement on receiving links was retrievable, so it should be validated with a few EA conversations rather than taken as fact. Calendly's counter-claim is an **83% completion rate** for people who click a link, a vendor statistic with unpublished methodology ([Calendly](https://calendly.com/resources/guides/state-of-scheduling-report)). Both can be true: most people complete, and the ones who resent it are the senior counterparts and their assistants.

## What to build: text first, an assistant mode, and graded availability

The evidence above resolves into a small number of design commitments. The table maps each to the complaint or praise it answers.

| Recommendation | What it answers |
|---|---|
| Keep Calendly's praised defaults: guest needs no account, sees only real availability in their own zone, both calendars update, 24h + 1h reminders, reply-to set to the booker | Uniform praise for "just works" and no-shows ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/calendly/reviews?qs=pros-and-cons); [nickgray.net](https://nickgray.net/calendly/)) |
| Lead every outreach with 3–6 pasted times in both zones; link second, framed as optional | Etiquette backlash, EA "paste your availability" norm ([Wes Kao](https://newsletter.weskao.com/p/unspoken-power-dynamics-of-calendly); [Overlappr](https://overlappr.com/blog/how-to-schedule-meetings-across-time-zones/)) |
| "I'm booking for someone else" mode: principal's details, cc field for the assistant, confirmation and manage link to the booker | Calendly's on-behalf notification gap ([Community 1069](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/managing-calendly-on-behalf-of-others-1069)) |
| Counter-proposal inside the flow, plus optional overlay of the guest's own calendar | "Get in line" critique; SavvyCal's collaborative framing ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-01-31/calendar-etiquette-calendly-auto-scheduling-soctware-cause-grievances); [SavvyCal](https://savvycal.com/calendly-vs-savvycal)) |
| Hold-friendly export: copy-as-text in both zones, per-option .ics "HOLD:" files, stated reservation window | EA hold convention and offered-time tracking ([SnackNation](https://snacknation.com/blog/calendar-management-for-executive-assistants/); [The EA Campus](https://theeacampus.com/blog/review-of-vimcal-ea/)) |
| Graded availability: hard blocks, soft blocks displaceable by tier, audience policies, adjacency rule | Busy/free reductionism and open tracker asks ([Calendly Help](https://calendly.com/help/how-to-manage-busy-vs-free-calendar-settings); [cal.com #26187](https://github.com/calcom/cal.com/issues/26187)) |
| Read every calendar; treat busy as authoritative; explain which rule hid a slot | Sync coverage complaints and the "no times available" genre ([Community 1154](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/calendar-sync-failing-getting-double-booked-1154); [Calendly Help](https://calendly.com/help/how-to-troubleshoot-unavailable-times-that-should-be-available)) |
| Zone in every artefact, never a silent UTC fallback; cancel and reschedule from the page | Wrong-hour confirmations; email-only cancel ([Calendly Help](https://calendly.com/help/time-zones-overview); [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/calendly/reviews/all)) |
| Any automation discloses itself, caps re-proposals, hands back when the other side stalls | x.ai and Howie lessons ([GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2015/testing-amy-what-its-like-to-have-appointments-scheduled-by-an-ai-assistant/); [The Split](https://www.thespl.it/p/howie-the-ai-secretary-austin-petersmith)) |

The availability model deserves the most design effort because it is where nothing on the market fits this reader. Replace the busy/free bit with at least three states read from every calendar the owner has, using event type where Google exposes it and title or colour conventions otherwise, since the `outOfOffice` and `focusTime` types are restricted to primary calendars and some accounts ([Google Developers](https://developers.google.com/workspace/calendar/api/guides/calendar-status)). A hard block is never offered to anyone: the recurring 3.15–4.00pm school-pickup band belongs here, as do hard personal commitments on a secondary calendar that Google's own appointment schedules would ignore. A soft block (focus time, a tentative hold, "probably free after 4.15") is offered only to requests above a priority threshold, which is Reclaim's priority-link idea made explicit and inspectable rather than enforced by moving things ([Reclaim Help](https://help.reclaim.ai/en/articles/8291694-how-reclaim-uses-priorities-to-intelligently-plan-your-workweek)). Layer audience policies on top, following SavvyCal's ranked schedules: friends may book evenings and weekends but not the working day; work contacts see 9.00–3.00 with mornings ranked first; strangers see only batched afternoon windows on days that already contain meetings, using the Cal.com adjacency proposal's parameters of tolerance, event filter and empty-day behaviour as a ready-made spec ([SavvyCal Docs](https://docs.savvycal.com/article/20-ranked-availability); [cal.com #26187](https://github.com/calcom/cal.com/issues/26187)). Because no tool recognises the booker's identity, implement audiences as separate links plus single-use links with one-off widened hours for the exceptions, rather than editing the standing schedule each time ([Calendly Help](https://calendly.com/help/how-to-create-a-single-use-link)). Then do the thing Calendly's owners spend a support genre wishing for: when a slot is hidden, the owner's view should say which calendar, rule or cap hid it.

The outreach layer should copy the EA loop rather than the Calendly page. The tool's primary output for a new request is a message, not a URL: a handful of concrete options written as "Tue 7 Oct, 10.00–10.30 BST / 05.00 ET", each already held on the owner's calendar with a self-cleaning hold in the Vimcal and Notion style, with a line stating how long the options are reserved and the booking page as the richer alternative ([Vimcal](https://www.vimcal.com/calendly-alternative); [Notion Help](https://www.notion.com/help/availability-blocking-and-time-zones)). This neutralises the "you do the work" reading, matches how the assistant on the other side already writes, and lets them drag a slot straight onto their principal's calendar. On the page itself, three things make it PA-friendly that no mainstream link product bundles: an explicit "I'm booking on behalf of someone" toggle that collects the principal's name and email, cc's the assistant on the invite and sends the assistant their own confirmation and manage link; a counter-propose control that lets the assistant reply with two or three times of their own in their zone, echoed back in both zones so the paraphrase failure that broke Amy cannot recur; and a stability guarantee that the times shown do not silently shift for the stated window. Keep the guest flow account-free, keep the first screen non-empty (owners report invitees never click to the next month when the current one shows nothing) ([Community 3252](https://community.calendly.com/how-do-i-40/enhancement-request-re-dealing-with-view-next-month-inertia-3252)), and request the narrowest calendar scopes that still let you read busy time across every calendar, since scope breadth is itself a complaint.

Two things not to build. Do not build an autonomous email negotiator; every survivor in that category kept humans reachable, and the failures recipients punish (not knowing they are talking to a bot, being re-pinged) are precisely the ones an accountable assistant on the other side will hold against the owner. And do not out-feature Calendly on team mechanics, routing, round robin or embeds; the praised core for a solo user is the professional page, time-zone handling, reminders and an optional intake question, and everything in the complaint clusters that is about pricing, seats and support simply does not exist for a personal tool.

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing from this research is that Calendly's two reputations are not in tension. People love it because the default path never fails and hate it because the default path is the only path: one availability surface, shown identically to everyone, with the recipient's only move being to comply. Every etiquette script, every SavvyCal or Reclaim feature, and every open Cal.com issue is an attempt to add a second path, whether ranked preference, counter-proposal, priority tiers or batching, and the vendors have left most of those open for years as low priority. That is a strong signal that a personal tool built around graded, audience-aware availability and a text-first, hold-friendly outreach flow is not reinventing a commodity but filling a gap the commodity vendors have chosen not to fill.

The largest remaining uncertainty is the assistant's own voice. The research could not reach a single working EA speaking about receiving links, and the "EAs prefer to coordinate" claim rests on vendor copy and etiquette commentary. Before investing in the assistant-mode features, the cheapest next step is three or four conversations with the PAs this reader already deals with, asking what they do with a link today, whether they would use a "booking for someone else" path, and whether pasted times with a stated hold window would change how they respond. Those answers will either confirm the design above or reveal that the assistants have quietly made their peace with links, in which case the effort should shift almost entirely to the availability model.
